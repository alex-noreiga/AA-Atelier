Pricing a Custom Piece

Cost it in the costing database, read the suggested price, then put it on the invoice.

Cost it

  1. Start a costing row

    In finances → costing, new row from the New custom work template. One row per piece, so a two-garment order gets two rows on the same order.

    Item
    Name it.
    Order
    Link to the order. Without this the invoice tool has nothing to read.
    Pricing Settings
    Custom / Direct. The template fills this in.
    Labor Hours
    Your estimate, and never leave it blank. The rate comes from the settings row, $5/hr right now.
    Channel
    Custom. This one is just a label, it does not affect the price.

  2. Add a usage line per material

    In production → material usage database, or from the costing row's Material Usage Lines field. There are templates for the usual ones: outer fabric, padding layer, absorbant fabric, blade cloth, bags, packaging label.

    Usage Line
    The name the client will see on the invoice.
    Material
    Link to materials inventory. Unit cost comes from its Price per Unit.
    Quantity Used
    In that material's own unit. Include waste.
    Usage Type
    Material, or Packaging to keep it in your cost but off the client's invoice.
    Costing Item
    Link back to the piece.

  3. Read the price

    Everything on the costing row calculates itself. Break Even Price is your floor, Suggested Price is the quote, Profit is what you keep. With the current settings, 100% margin and no fees on Custom / Direct, the suggested price lands at twice materials plus labor plus packaging.

    To move it: change Labor Hours for this piece, or the margin on the Pricing Settings row, which moves everything using that row. Never type a price into the costing or the invoice.

The two things that quietly underprice a piece

Empty Labor Hours prices the piece at materials plus margin with the labor left out, and it does it silently. And an invoice built by hand out of material lines bills those materials at cost, with no labor and no margin at all. Let the itemize tool write the lines.

The two Pricing Settings rows

Custom / Direct charges no selling fee. Production / Marketplace adds 6.5%, for pieces sold somewhere that takes a cut. Custom work belongs on the first, and the Channel select does not decide this, the relation does.

Bill it

  1. Deposits

    On the order's invoice in invoices & payments, put a number in First Deposit Amount. That alone makes it payable online. Same for Second Deposit Amount at the fitting. Add First Deposit Due, Second Deposit Due, or Payment Deadline to turn on reminder emails for those stages.

  2. Itemize

    /studio → Itemize an invoice, type the order number. It writes a material line per usage line, a labor line, a design & finishing line for the margin, and a rush surcharge if the order is marked rush. Totals to the suggested price.

    It runs once. If the invoice already has lines it does nothing, so to redo it after changing the costing, delete the lines in Notion and run it again.

  3. Invoice Ready

    Tick it and the client gets the balance and the itemized invoice. The paid checkboxes look after themselves from there, unless someone pays you in person.

If the price looks wrong

What you seeWhat it is
Roughly 7% high Pricing Settings is on Production / Marketplace. Switch to Custom / Direct.
Suggested Price blank or tiny No Pricing Settings link, or Labor Hours is empty.
Invoice is about the cost of the fabric The lines were added by hand, so there is no labor line and no margin. Delete them and run the itemize tool.
Material Cost is missing a material That usage line is not linked to the costing item, or the material has no Price per Unit.
Packaging is showing on the client's invoice Its Usage Type is Material, not Packaging.
Invoice totals more than the quote A hand-added invoice line is linked to a Costing Item, which prices at the whole garment. Clear the link or delete the line.
Changed the costing, invoice unchanged Lines are only written once. Delete them and rerun the itemize tool.
costing and invoices & payments live under finances. Material usage lives under production. Rates and margin live on the two Pricing Settings rows.
