The short version

How it works

You build the invoice in Notion. The website reads it and collects the money through Stripe. The site never sets a price of its own and never edits your costing. If a number is wrong on the site, it is wrong in Notion.

Two databases under finances do the work:

  • invoices & payments: one row per order. This is the invoice itself. It holds the two deposit amounts, the paid checkboxes, the due dates, and the Invoice Ready switch.
  • Invoice Line Items: the itemized lines that add up to the subtotal. Each line is a Garment, Material, Labor, Adjustment, or Surcharge.

The invoice and the order are joined by a relation: Order on the invoice, Invoices on the order. The site follows the order's Invoices field to find the invoice, so after linking them, glance at the order row and confirm the invoice shows up there.

The only things the site ever writes to the invoice are the paid checkboxes, the Stripe session id next to each one, the reminder checkboxes, and the invoice title when you run the itemize tool. Your costing formulas are never touched.

What a client pays, and when

The three payments

First deposit
Usually taken once the sketch is settled. You type the amount.
No tax
Second deposit
Usually taken at the first fitting. You type the amount.
No tax
Final balance
Line items minus the deposits already marked paid. Calculated, never typed.
Tax added

A deposit becomes payable online the moment you put an amount in its field. The client does not have to wait for the itemized invoice.

The balance is the sum of the line items minus every deposit that is marked paid, and it never goes below zero. It only appears to the client after you tick Invoice Ready. Sales tax is added at checkout on the balance only, which is why that checkout asks for a billing address. Deposits stay untaxed on purpose.

There is no fixed order

A stage is payable whenever it has an amount and is not already marked paid. The client can pay the second deposit first, or the balance after only one deposit. The balance always subtracts whatever is marked paid at that moment, so the totals stay right either way.

Stripe's promo code and gift card box is on all three checkouts, so a code you create in the Stripe dashboard works on a deposit or the balance. If installment payments (Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay) are turned on, they show on the balance only. Deposits are card only.

Step by step, in Notion

Setting up an invoice

  1. Link the invoice to the order

    In invoices & payments, find or create the row for this order and set its Order relation. One invoice per order. Check the order row now shows the invoice under Invoices, because that is the side the website reads.

  2. Set the first deposit

    Type the number into First Deposit Amount. That is all it takes for the client to be able to pay it online. Add First Deposit Due if you want a reminder email to go out before the date (see reminders).

  3. Set the second deposit

    Same thing, in Second Deposit Amount and Second Deposit Due.

  4. Itemize the invoice

    Run the Itemize an invoice tool on the studio dashboard at /studio. It writes the material, labor, and design lines from the order's costing. Details are in the next section. You can also add or edit lines by hand in Invoice Line Items.

  5. Set the payment deadline

    Payment Deadline is the balance's due date. The client sees it on their invoice page, and it is what triggers the balance reminder email.

  6. Tick Invoice Ready

    Now the balance and the View invoice button appear for the client. Until then they see the deposits only, and the invoice page tells them the invoice is not ready yet.

  7. Leave the paid boxes alone

    When a client pays, the matching Paid box ticks itself within a few seconds, and the Stripe session id lands next to it. Tick a box yourself only for a payment that came in outside the website.

The dashboard tool

Itemizing from the costing

Sign in at /studio, scroll to the tools, type the order number into Itemize an invoice, and press Itemize. It reads the order's costing items and writes:

  • One Material line per material usage line, priced at that line's material cost. Packaging usage lines are skipped, since that is an internal cost.
  • One Labor line at the total labor cost from the costing.
  • One Adjustment line called "Design & finishing" that makes the itemized total land exactly on the costing's suggested price. This is where the margin sits.
  • A Surcharge line if the order has Rush Order ticked, at 15% of the itemized subtotal.

It also renames the invoice to the order number. Then it tells you what it wrote and what the invoice totals.

It runs once

If the invoice already has any line items, the tool adds nothing and says so. That is deliberate, so a second press cannot duplicate the invoice. To rebuild it after changing the costing, delete the existing lines in Notion and run it again.

If the order has no costing items linked, the tool cannot run and tells you that. Link the costing to the order first.

Why not add a costing line by hand

A costing item is a whole-garment price. Its suggested price already includes materials, labor, and margin. If you add a line linked to the costing item and also add material and labor lines, the client is billed for the same work twice. The tool avoids this by pricing every line it writes manually and never linking the costing item. Follow the same rule with any line you add yourself.

Three things worth knowing

Rules that keep the math right

A ticked Paid box hides the pay button

The site treats Paid as the record of payment. Tick it before the money arrives and the client can no longer pay that stage online, and the balance drops by that amount. Untick it and the pay option comes back. Recording a real payment taken outside the site is the one time you tick it yourself.

Deposits are credits, not line items

Deposits live on the invoice row, never in Invoice Line Items. Do not create a "Deposit" line type. A deposit line would bill the client for their own deposit, and it would also throw off Notion's Final Balance, which does not filter those out.

The balance is calculated, not stored

The client is charged the sum of the line items minus the deposits marked paid, recalculated every time the page loads. The Final Balance field in Notion is for your reading. The site does its own sum so a stray line cannot overcharge anyone.

Sent for you, overnight

Automatic payment reminders

If a stage has a due date, is unpaid, and the date is within seven days or already past, the nightly job emails the client a reminder with the amount and a link to pay. It covers all three stages: First Deposit Due, Second Deposit Due, and Payment Deadline for the balance.

  • A stage with no due date is never reminded about. That is the switch: set a date to turn reminders on for that stage.
  • Each stage gets one reminder, ever. After it sends, the matching Reminded checkbox is ticked so the job does not send it again night after night. Untick it if you want another one to go out.
  • Nothing goes to the client for a stage already marked paid.
  • You do not have to wait for the overnight run. Reconcile production milestones on the dashboard does the same sweep on demand.
The other side of the screen

What the client sees

The client goes to the Track page and enters their order number, or opens the order from their account if they have one. From there:

  • Deposit cards. Every deposit with an amount on it shows as a card with a Pay button that opens Stripe checkout. Once paid, the card reads "paid" and offers a receipt link.
  • The balance. After Invoice Ready is ticked, a card shows the balance due (or "Paid in full") with a View invoice button.
  • The invoice page. The lines grouped under Garment, Materials, Labor, Adjustments, and Surcharge, then the subtotal, each deposit as a credit, and the balance due. There is a Pay balance button and a Download PDF button.

Deposits show up before the invoice is itemized. The balance shows up only after you tick Invoice Ready. That gap is the point: you can collect a deposit while you are still costing the garment out.

An unpaid deposit is listed on the invoice page as a credit of $0.00 with "(unpaid)" next to it, so the arithmetic reads clearly. It turns into a real credit once it is paid.

Troubleshooting

When something looks wrong

The client says they cannot pay the balance

Check Invoice Ready is ticked. If it is, check the line items add up to more than the deposits already marked paid, since a balance of zero has no pay button. Also check Balance Paid is not already ticked.

A deposit is not showing for the client

A deposit only appears once its amount is a number above zero. A blank field or a 0 is skipped entirely.

It says a payment is paid, but nobody paid

A Paid box is ticked when it should not be. Untick it and the pay option comes back.

The itemize tool said it added nothing

The invoice already has line items, so it left them alone. Delete the lines in Notion and run it again to rebuild from the current costing.

I need to change a price after the invoice is out

Edit the line in Invoice Line Items. The balance recalculates on its own, live, and the client sees the new figure on their next page load. Nothing else to do, as long as they have not already paid the balance.

They paid me by e-transfer, cash, or in person

The site only knows about payments made through Stripe. Tick that stage's Paid box yourself, and type IN_PERSON into that stage's Session Id field so the record shows where the money came from. The client's card will read "paid" with no receipt link, which is expected, since there is no Stripe receipt to link to.

The order is canceled and I need to refund them

Use Cancel & refund an order on the studio dashboard rather than refunding in Stripe by hand. It refunds every paid stage on the invoice, marks the order cancelled, and emails the client. Payments already refunded are left alone, so running it twice is safe.

Line types in Invoice Line Items

  • Garment, Material, Labor, Adjustment, and Surcharge all add up to the subtotal, and all show on the client's invoice under those headings.
  • There is no Deposit line type, on purpose. Deposits belong on the invoice row.
Formulas for your eyes only

Paid to Date, Remaining to Collect, and Payment Status are there for you to read. The website does not use any of them. Payment Status flips to overdue at midnight on a payment's due date.
